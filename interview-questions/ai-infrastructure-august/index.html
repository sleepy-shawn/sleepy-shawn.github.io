AI Infrastructure Interview Notes from August

November 16, 2026 · 2 min · Shuai

This post is just a personal record of where I fell short in the interview. It isn’t meant to serve as a reference.

I had the interview in August and am only now looking back and writing it up. The interviewer was excellent, with a strong grounding in AI infrastructure from operators all the way up to frameworks. (I’ll admit it: I’m a Kimi fanboy.) All the questions were based on my past experience.

  1. How does the all-reduce algorithm work?

  2. How can you express all-reduce as reduce-scatter followed by all-gather?

  3. How does DeepEP handle communication for expert parallelism (EP)?

  4. What are the two approaches to EP?

  5. Roughly estimate the communication volume for EP.

  6. Suppose the preceding attention layer uses tensor parallelism (TP), so the output projection (o_proj) is followed by an all-reduce. There are two ways to run EP after that:

    1. Every GPU has all the tokens. At the end of EP, just run an all-reduce.
    2. Use reduce-scatter after the TP output projection so each GPU has only a subset of the tokens. Then follow DeepEP’s dispatch-and-combine approach for EP, and use all-gather when returning to attention in the next layer.

    How do these two approaches differ? Estimate the communication volume in the decoding setting as the batch size starts at 1 and gradually increases.

    This was complicated. I spent a long time working through it, and there were many factors to consider beyond communication volume.

  7. What is the NVFP4 format?

  8. Have you written a dequantization kernel? What are the key considerations?

  9. When adapting to NVFP4, how do you handle the global scale together with the fused MoE kernel?

  10. What is the principle behind Hadamard rotation?

  11. Besides using the Hadamard transform for weight GEMMs, can you use it in attention?

  12. How does FlashAttention-3 apply the Hadamard transform to attention?

  13. Coding exercise: Implement Spiral Matrix from scratch.
