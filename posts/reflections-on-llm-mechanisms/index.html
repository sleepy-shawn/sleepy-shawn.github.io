Reflections on an LLM Mechanisms Lecture

One impression from the lecture was that research on LLM mechanisms can require less compute and more careful reasoning.

Scaling Laws#

  • Recasting a discrete problem in continuous form can simplify the analysis.
  • The lecture discussed the warmup–stable–decay (WSD) learning rate schedule. Training dynamics describe how a system evolves over time.
  • One limitation is how well the results transfer across model sizes.

Optimization#

Why do we still use Adam when so many optimizers are available? The starting point was to understand both how an optimizer works and why it behaves as it does.

  • The intuition discussed in the lecture was that curvature affects optimization speed.
  • Adam did not converge in the quadratic example discussed in the lecture. The example used high-precision Python arithmetic and set epsilon = 0.
  • In the curvature degradation example, momentum did not have the expected effect, while Adam did.
  • Other topics included loss spikes, SignGD, and adaptive Muon. SignGD uses the sign of the gradient and discards its magnitude. Adaptive Muon was mentioned as a possible direction for future work.

Looped Transformers#

The goals were stable training and zero-shot parameter transfer. Work on μP focuses on transferring training dynamics. The lecture discussed a surge in the residual stream when looping and highlighted two quantities to tune: the residual coefficient and learning rate scaling.

One question was whether improvements from looping come from differences in computation. The comparison tracked FLOPs, total parameter count, and KV cache usage. To make the comparison fair, the experiments reduced model width and adjusted the scheduling of experts and attention. The lecture also discussed the optimal aspect ratio. Two loops gave the best result among the configurations discussed.

  • The scaling law was extended to mixture-of-experts (MoE) models. CE gain was used to compare the benefits of different architectures.
  • With similar training loss trajectories, the looped Transformer performed better across the benchmarks discussed. The gain was larger on longer, structured text, and in-context learning further improved its advantage.
  • DataComp for Language Models (DCLM) focuses on the quality of pretraining data. DataComp-LM
  • The attention patterns appeared similar to the baseline, but the models read information differently. One question was the role of attention sinks, where a position receives a large share of attention despite carrying little useful information.

Quadratic Models#

The lecture explored how to move from toy models toward more general models. Topics included learning rate schedules, data mixing under a fixed budget, and power spectrum analysis. The discussion emphasized that loss during mid-training matters as much as loss during pretraining.

Hessian spectrum analysis illustrated the limitations of a quadratic approximation. Other topics included the edge-of-stability phenomenon, the neural tangent kernel (NTK) approximation, feature loss, and kernel loss.

Pretraining Beyond Tiny Language Models#

  • Open training recipes and pipelines.
  • Puro’s cost scaling law and ways to reduce the cost of experiments.
  • FP8 support on the Blackwell-based RTX 5090.
  • Muon Hyperball, which combines Muon, projection, and control of the effective learning rate.
  • Multiple power laws, and Hunyuan’s shift from learning rate to effective learning rate (ELR).
  • The 2022 Chinchilla scaling law. Training Compute-Optimal Large Language Models

A learning rate that is too small can hide the benefits of a good idea.

Auto-Bidding#

The lecture described two earlier approaches: solver-driven methods and learned policies such as Decision Transformers, which are limited by the data available to them.

Why use an LLM for auto-bidding? One concern is that LLMs lack numerical precision. This raises a more specific question: how precise does a bidding decision need to be? The perturbation experiment discussed in the lecture suggested that the decisions were relatively robust. Discretization and sequential decision-making remain challenges.

The system prompt includes the current state, insights from theory, and short-term memory covering the three most recent steps.

Discussion#

Curiosity was one reason people chose this direction. Other paths into the work included questions about model architecture, difficulties with diffusion language models that led to theoretical research, and work on AI safety and adversarial image classification that led to scaling laws. Lil’Log and questions about model size, data size, and learning rates were also mentioned. Several of these questions came from practice.

The discussion returned to the gap between theory and applications. A deeper understanding can inform real applications. For theoretical research, the speakers emphasized finding a good question, potentially from industry, and using scientific reasoning to guide the work. They placed less emphasis on mathematical technique alone.

The closing observation was that agentic mechanisms may require substantially larger models.
