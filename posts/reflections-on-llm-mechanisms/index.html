This domain requires less compute but more thinking.

Scaling Laws#

  • Tricks: Convert a discrete form to a continuous formulation to simplify the analysis.
  • Knowledge: The WSD learning rate mechanism. Dynamics describe how a system evolves over time.
  • Limitation: Transfer across model sizes.

Optimization#

  • The question is: Why do we still use Adam when there are so many optimizers?
  • Their fundamental starting point is to understand how and why.
  • Intuitively, the magnitude of curvature is proportional to optimization speed.
  • In the quadratic function setting, Adam does not converge.
  • Use a high-precision Python library and set epsilon = 0.
  • In the curvature degradation problem, momentum does not have the expected effect, but Adam does.
  • The loss spike problem.
  • SignGD uses the sign of the gradient without its magnitude.
  • Future: Adaptive Muon.

Looped Transformer#

  • Goals: Stability and zero-shot hyperparameter transfer.
  • Work in the μP series focuses on dynamics transfer.
  • Loop residual surge.
  • Two parameters: The residual coefficient and the scaling of learning rates.
  • The argument focuses on whether the improvement of the looped Transformer comes from improvements in computation.
  • The metrics are FLOPs, total parameters, and KV cache usage.
  • To make the comparison equal, the width will be reduced, and the experts and attention will be rescheduled.
  • They care about the optimal aspect ratio.
  • The best result occurs with two loops.
  • The scaling law extends to MoE.
  • CE gain measures the benefits of different architectures.
  • With the same loss trajectories during training, the looped Transformer shows its advantage across benchmarks.
  • The gain is more significant in longer, structured text.
  • In-context learning further improves these advantages.
  • DCLM stands for DataComp for Language Models and focuses on the quality of pretraining data.
  • Attention looks the same but reads differently from the baseline.
  • Attention sinks? They accumulate a large amount of attention mass without carrying much important information.

Quadratic Model#

  • From toy models to universal models.
  • Learning rate schedule.
  • The data mixing problem under a budget.
  • Power spectrum analysis.
  • Mid-training loss is as important as pretraining loss.
  • Hessian spectrum analysis.
  • The quadratic model is not good enough.
  • The edge-of-stability phenomenon.
  • NTK: Neural tangent kernel approximation.
  • Feature loss and kernel loss.

Pretraining Beyond a Tiny Language Model#

  • Open recipe and open pipeline.
  • Puro’s cost scaling law.
  • Reduce costs.
  • FP8 is another advantage of the RTX 5090’s Blackwell architecture.
  • Muon Hyperball = Muon + projection + effective learning rate control.
  • Multiple power laws.
  • Effective learning rate: Hunyuan’s shift from LR to ELR.
  • The 2022 Chinchilla scaling law by Google.
  • A small learning rate underuses a good idea.

Auto-Bidding#

  • Two earlier paradigms: Solver-driven methods and learned policies limited by data, such as Decision Transformers.
  • Why not use LLMs in auto-bidding?
  • Some people think LLMs lack numerical precision.
  • How precise should this bidding decision be?
  • The perturbation experiment shows its relative robustness.
  • Discretization and the challenge of sequential decision-making.

The harness includes:

  • The system prompt.
  • The current state.
  • Theoretical guidance through insight injection.
  • Short-term memory (STM) for the three most recent steps.

Discussions#

Why Choose This Direction?#

  • Curiosity.
  • Questions about architecture.
  • Diffusion language models did not work, which led to theory.
  • AI safety and adversarial image classifiers led to scaling laws. Scaling is magical.
  • Scaling laws, Lil’Log, model size and data size, and then learning rates. The motivation came from practice.

The Gap Between Applications and Theory?#

  • A deep understanding can lead to real applications.
  • The key abilities for theoretical research are understanding a good question, possibly from industry, and using scientific reasoning to guide the research. Mathematical skills are less important than before.
  • Agentic mechanisms require larger models, quite large ones.
