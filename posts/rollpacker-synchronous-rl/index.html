Paper: RollPacker: Taming Long-Tail Rollouts for RL Post-Training with Tail Batching

The Long-Tail Distribution Problem#

The long-tail distribution of response lengths remains a problem.

Tail Batching#

A rollout stage may fall short of the number of prompts or responses required to form an effective batch. RollPacker addresses this through speculative execution: it overprovisions requests while selectively retaining only the fastest completions. Perhaps this idea is inspired by speculative decoding.

Systematically excluding long prompts distorts the training sample distribution and may harm model performance. My understanding is that preserving the original sample distribution helps avoid this issue.

During stream execution, gradients for completed responses are computed and buffered, but no updates are applied to the model parameters or optimizer state.

Adaptive Parallelism#

Short rounds impose greater GPU memory pressure because speculative execution launches more concurrent requests, potentially triggering preemption. Increasing the tensor-parallel (TP) size can mitigate this pressure, but it also introduces communication overhead.

RollPacker profiles memory usage offline and selects the optimal TP configuration online.

To limit cross-node communication, TP groups are confined to a single GPU server.

Reward Scheduler#

The reward scheduler operates asynchronously. Its optimizations include:

  • Adjusting sandbox timeouts.
  • Dynamically sharing GPUs for judge models. Most layers of the judge LLM are offloaded to host memory, and their parameters are streamed over PCIe in coordination with activation computation on the GPU.

Stream Trainer#

Some GPUs may become idle when their assigned requests finish early. These rollout GPUs can then be repurposed for training.

A heuristic algorithm decides when to reassign GPUs and which GPUs to reassign. This allows training and rollout to overlap.

To preserve training semantics, the stream trainer adjusts the loss scales and defers gradient updates until streaming concludes.
